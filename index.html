2. Thermochemical Foundations & Energy Kinetics

Biomass consists of three primary organic biopolymers: Cellulose, Hemicellulose, and Lignin. During burning, fuel transitions through four distinct thermal stages.

Biomass Structural Polymers

Photosynthesis stores solar radiation as chemical energy:

$$6 CO_2 + 6 H_2O + h\nu \longrightarrow C_6H_{12}O_6 + 6 O_2 \quad \text{}$$
  • Cellulose ($(C_6H_{10}O_5)_n$, 40–50% dry wt): Linear polymer of D-glucose. Undergoes rapid devolatilization at $280^\circ\text{C} - 380^\circ\text{C}$.
  • Hemicellulose (20–30% dry wt): Branched polysaccharides. Early decomposition at $200^\circ\text{C} - 260^\circ\text{C}$ releasing light volatile gas.
  • Lignin (15–30% dry wt): Complex aromatic network. Decomposes slowly across $200^\circ\text{C} - 900^\circ\text{C}$, creating the durable char matrix for glowing embers.

3. Mathematical Model Formulations & System Derivations

Complete mathematical proofs for water evaporation energy penalty, Higher Heating Value (HHV), lumped heat dynamics, and Circular Energy Return Index (CERI).

3.1 Comprehensive Derivation of Water Evaporation Thermal Loss ($Q_{\text{evap}}$)

When moist fuel burns, internal water acts as a massive thermal heat sink. The thermal energy consumed ($Q_{\text{evap}}$) across three distinct physical phases is derived step-by-step:

PHASE 1: Sensible Heating of Liquid Water $$Q_{\text{sensible, liquid}} = m_{\text{water}} \cdot c_{p,l} \cdot (T_b - T_0) \quad \text{}$$
PHASE 2: Latent Heat of Vaporization $$Q_{\text{latent}} = m_{\text{water}} \cdot h_{fg} \quad \text{}$$
PHASE 3: Sensible Heating of Vapor $$Q_{\text{sensible, vapor}} = m_{\text{water}} \cdot c_{p,v} \cdot (T_{\text{flue}} - T_b) \quad \text{}$$

Summing all three phases and substituting $m_{\text{water}} = M \cdot m_{\text{fuel}}$:

$$Q_{\text{evap}} = M \cdot m_{\text{fuel}} \left[ c_{p,l}(100 - T_0) + h_{fg} + c_{p,v}(T_{\text{flue}} - 100) \right] \quad (\text{in kJ}) \quad \text{}$$

Inserting standard thermodynamic constants ($T_0 = 25^\circ\text{C}$, $T_{\text{flue}} = 200^\circ\text{C}$, $c_{p,l} = 4.184 \text{ kJ/kg}\cdot\text{K}$, $h_{fg} = 2260 \text{ kJ/kg}$, $c_{p,v} = 1.996 \text{ kJ/kg}\cdot\text{K}$):

$$\boxed{Q_{\text{evap}} = 2773.4 \cdot M \cdot m_{\text{fuel}} \quad (\text{in kJ})} \quad \text{}$$

Physical Meaning: Every $1\text{ kg}$ of moisture absorbs $\approx 2.77\text{ MJ}$ of useful thermal heat purely to evaporate and expand, cooling the fire and creating heavy smoke soot!

3.2 HHV & Net LHV Formulation

Dulong's correlation based on elemental ultimate analysis:

$$\boxed{HHV_{\text{dry}} = 0.3383 \cdot w_C + 1.422 \cdot \left(w_H - \frac{w_O}{8}\right) - 0.0153 \cdot w_A \quad (\text{MJ/kg})} \quad \text{}$$ $$\boxed{LHV_{\text{net}} = HHV_{\text{dry}} \cdot (1 - M) - 2.442 \cdot M \quad (\text{MJ/kg})} \quad \text{}$$

3.3 Lumped Thermal Bed Differential Model

First Law energy balance governing temperature rate change $\frac{dT(t)}{dt}$:

$$\boxed{\frac{dT(t)}{dt} = \frac{\eta_c \dot{m}_f(t) LHV_{\text{net}} - h_c A_s (T(t) - T_{\text{amb}}) - \epsilon \sigma A_s (T(t)^4 - T_{\text{amb}}^4) - \dot{Q}_{\text{evap}}}{m_{\text{sys}} c_{p,\text{bed}}}} \quad \text{}$$

At peak thermal equilibrium ($\frac{dT(t)}{dt} = 0$), peak flame temperature is:

$$\boxed{T_{\text{peak}} = T_{\text{amb}} + \frac{\eta_c \cdot \dot{m}_{f,\text{peak}} \cdot LHV_{\text{net}} - \dot{Q}_{\text{evap}}}{h_{\text{eff}} \cdot A_s}} \quad \text{}$$

3.4 Counter-Flow Heat Exchanger & Circular Energy Return Index (CERI)

Heat Recovery Model ($\epsilon_{\text{HEX}}$)

$$Q_{\text{recovered}} = \epsilon_{\text{HEX}} \cdot \dot{m}_{\text{flue}} \cdot c_{p,\text{flue}} \cdot (T_{\text{flue,in}} - T_{\text{cold,in}}) \quad (\text{kW}) \quad \text{}$$ $$E_{\text{recovered}} = \int_{0}^{t_{\text{burn}}} Q_{\text{recovered}}(t) \, dt \quad (\text{kJ}) \quad \text{}$$

Circular Energy Return Index (CERI) Formula

$$\boxed{\text{CERI} = \frac{\text{Useful Thermal Outputs}}{\text{Lifecycle Inputs}} = \frac{Q_{\text{cook}} + E_{\text{recovered}} + E_{\text{ash}}}{E_{\text{press}} + E_{\text{thermal, dry}}}} \quad \text{}$$

5. Machine Learning & Multi-Objective Pareto Optimization

Gaussian Process Regression (GPR) models non-linear fuel interactions using a Matérn $5/2$ covariance kernel.

Matérn 5/2 Covariance Kernel Equation

$$\boxed{k_{\text{Matérn } 5/2}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{x}') = \sigma_f^2 \left( 1 + \frac{\sqrt{5}d}{l} + \frac{5d^2}{3l^2} \right) \exp\left( -\frac{\sqrt{5}d}{l} \right)} \quad \text{}$$

Where $d = \|\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}'\|$ is Euclidean distance between vector inputs $\mathbf{x} = [w_{\text{STW}}, w_{\text{RH}}, w_{\text{Binder}}, M, P_{\text{press}}]^T$.

Vector Multi-Objective Optimization

$$\min_{\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{X}} \mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x}) = \left[ -f_{\text{CERI}}(\mathbf{x}), -T_{\text{peak}}(\mathbf{x}), f_{PM2.5}(\mathbf{x}), f_{CO}(\mathbf{x}) \right]^T \quad \text{}$$

import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

# Dynamic Combustion Profile Simulation
time_sec = np.linspace(0, 60, 360)
temp_profile = 25 + 580 * (1 - np.exp(-time_sec/6.5)) * np.exp(-time_sec/52.0)
pm_profile = 15 + 83 * np.exp(-time_sec/2.5) + 22 * np.exp(-(time_sec-12)**2 / 18.0)

fig, ax1 = plt.subplots(figsize=(7, 4.5), dpi=300)
ax2 = ax1.twinx()

p1, = ax1.plot(time_sec, temp_profile, 'r-', label='Flame Temp T(t) (°C)')
p2, = ax2.plot(time_sec, pm_profile, 'k--', label='PM2.5 Soot Index')

ax1.set_xlabel('Time (minutes)')
ax1.set_ylabel('Flame Core Temp (°C)', color='r')
ax2.set_ylabel('PM2.5 Soot Index', color='k')
ax1.set_title('Dynamic Combustion Trajectory (Run-05)')
ax1.grid(True)
plt.show()
